**Read this first.** Of 189 seeded `sources` rows, **114 are simulated placeholders** (`provenance = 'placeholder'`, `is_simulated = true`): every LGU water-truck drop point and every "neighboring barangay" row. The other 75 are real facilities from the WSP or OpenStreetMap, but their **travel times, prices and safety scores are our assumptions** (see Rubric). No real refill-station price, rationing point or trucking route for Catbalogan was found.

Mapping to barangays: each barangay with a centroid in `barangays.sql` (now all 57, see geocoding below) gets the nearest refill station, plus the second one if its round trip is <= 30 min, and the permissive well if its round trip is <= 15 min. Distance = haversine from the barangay centroid.

**Geocoding (Oct 6, 2026).** The 31 unserved barangays were geocoded with Nominatim (`<official name>, Catbalogan, Samar`, 1 request/s, result accepted only if `display_name` contains "Catbalogan"): **31 of 31 hits, 0 misses**, so none keeps the 40-min default. Values are approximate OSM centroids (header of `barangays.sql`), not survey points, and some are island or upland barangays (Bagongon, Cagutsan, Canhawan Gote, Cinco, Mombon, Rama, Buluan are 20+ km west of Poblacion across water), so the on-foot model overstates what is walkable: unserved refill round trips range 46 to 862 min (median 387), unserved neighbor rows 29 to 784. They are kept and flagged (spec 04 AC3), and the ranking puts them last. These barangays now also get the second refill station (if <= 30 min) and the permissive well (if <= 15 min) when in range, and a distance-based neighbor row.

## 2. Rubric and assumptions (ASSUMPTIONS, not measurements)

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| safety_score | refill station (treated, DOH-regulated) 0.9; LGU/BFP trucked chlorinated water 0.8; neighboring barangay still on piped supply 0.85; communal chlorinated deep well (WSP) 0.7; untreated spring or open well 0.4 |
| travel_minutes | on-foot round trip from the barangay centroid: `round(2 × haversine_km × 1.3 detour ÷ 4 km/h × 60)`, minimum 1. Compared against the WHO/UNICEF JMP 30-minute round-trip benchmark (spec 04) |
| trucking | fixed 10 min to the drop point |
| default A: 10 min | in-barangay point whose exact location is unknown (Lagundi hall ATM, Darahuway Cogao points) |
| default B: 40 min | barangay centroid unknown (all 31 unserved barangays). Rural, upland or island barangays, so a rough, **unverified** guess. It flags every unserved refill row as above the JMP limit |
| cost_php_per_unit | unit = one 5-gal container. Refill PHP 25 (estimate); trucked, communal and neighbor 0 |
| neighbor rows | nearest other CWD Level III barangay by centroid. This assumes the neighbor still has water, which is **false in a plant-wide Kulador failure** (all 26 served barangays share one network). **Ranking rule (spec 04, Dev A decision):** `neighboring_barangay` rows are excluded by `rank-chain` when the disruption cause is `turbidity` or `drought` (blended network, WSP pp.12, 15) and included only for `repair`; the exclusion is listed in the response's `excluded` array |

Caveats: the walking model ignores terrain, the sea (Darahuway is an island pair, Payao/Lagundi/Maulong are far from Poblacion), and open hours. Round trips of 100+ minutes for the Level I barangays mean the OSM refill rows are not realistically usable on foot there; they are kept because the JMP flag is the point of the model, and the ranking puts them last. Centroids are approximate Nominatim values (all 57).

## 4. Recommended next step for Teammate 3 (on-the-ground verification)

Priority order:
1. **Level I barangays** (Payao, Lagundi, Darahuway Gote, Darahuway Daco): confirm the ATM and tap points exist, who may draw from them, hours, and whether the Cogao line works when the CWD network is down. Payao's well is documented for government facilities only.
2. **Far or low-pressure zones**: Maulong, Mercedes, Bunu-anan, Guinsorongan, San Andres, Canlapwas. WSP pp.17, 27-28 name Zones 8 and 10, Maulong, Mercedes, Brgy. 13 and Bunu-anan as low or zero pressure at peak, and these are also far from the two OSM refill stations.
3. **Refill stations**: visit the two OSM stations (confirm operating, DOH permit, price per 5 gal) and ask the City Health Office / DTI for the full permit list. OSM almost certainly misses many stations.
4. **Rationing and trucking**: ask the CDRRMO, BFP and the CWD where water was actually distributed in July 2026 (read the PIA article that returned 403), then replace the simulated truck rows.
5. **Unserved barangays**: replace the OSM centroids with purok anchors or the barangay hall, and find real nearest sources and road access (island barangays need boat trips, not walking).
6. After verification: set `provenance`, `source_ref` and `is_simulated = false` on corrected rows, or add new `web` rows. The seed is regenerated from `gen_sources.py`; edit rows there.
